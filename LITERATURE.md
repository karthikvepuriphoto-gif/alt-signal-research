# Literature review (what published research says, and what we found)

Links marked ✔ were opened/verified during this research run (2026-10-23). Others are standard citations written from the literature; the DOI is given for convenience, so double-check it before you cite it formally.

## A. El Niño / La Niña (ENSO)
| Paper | Main finding | Our test |
|---|---|---|
| Cashin, Mohaddes & Raissi, *Fair Weather or Foul? The Macroeconomic Effects of El Niño*, IMF WP/15/89 (2015) ✔ https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/wp/2015/wp1589.pdf ; J. Int'l Econ. 106 (2017) ✔ https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinteco.2017.01.010 | 21-country GVAR, 1979–2013. El Niño shock: short-lived GDP fall in **Australia, Chile, Indonesia, India, Japan, NZ, South Africa**; growth *boost* for US/Europe; real **non-fuel commodity prices +5.3% and oil +13.9% after 4 quarters**; inflation up in most countries. | Our real-time version finds the *opposite sign* for grains: once El Niño is publicly known, wheat/maize prices tend to **fall** over the next months. That fits the move happening *while* the event develops (contemporaneous, which their VAR captures) and then reversing. |
| Brunner (2002), *El Niño and World Primary Commodity Prices: Warm Water or Hot Air?* Rev. Econ. Stat. 84(1) https://doi.org/10.1162/003465302317331991 | A 1-SD ENSO shock raises real commodity-price inflation by ~3.5–4 pp in the same year; ENSO explains ~20% of commodity-price inflation movement. | Same as above. The tradeable, lagged signal has the opposite sign. |
| Ubilava (2018), *The Role of ENSO in Commodity Price Movement and Predictability*, AJAE 100(1) https://doi.org/10.1093/ajae/aax060 | ENSO has regime-dependent effects on a set of commodity prices, and it modestly improves forecasts. | Consistent: the effects show up mainly in 2000–2026, not before. |
| Iizumi et al. (2014), *Impacts of ENSO on the global yields of major crops*, Nature Comms 5:3712 https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms4712 | El Niño hurts yields of Australian/Indian wheat and US/global soy *mixed*; La Niña tends to hurt US/Argentine maize & soy. | La Niña → **higher** maize/corn and crude prices (PPI corn, IMF maize) matches the La Niña-hurts-maize channel. |
| Ginn (2024), *Does ENSO Impact Equity Returns? Evidence via Country and Panel Regression*, Int'l Econ. J. 38(2) ✔ https://doi.org/10.1080/10168737.2024.2320116 | 20 economies: delayed, oscillating effect. Equity returns are positive in El Niño phases and negative in La Niña phases. | We find weak country effects. Mexico (EWW) is the one that holds up OOS (negative after El Niño). Australia/Brazil/India/Peru did not survive. |
| Yang (2017), *Essays on the El Niño Anomaly and Stock Return Predictability*, ODU dissertation ✔ https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1010&context=finance_etds | El Niño predicts returns in 14 countries (mostly in winter) and in 27 of 49 US industries. | Our FF-49 industry tests were mostly null, apart from coal/oil/steel under La Niña. Many industry claims in this literature look like multiple-testing artifacts. |
| Hong, Li & Xu (2019), *Climate risks and market efficiency*, J. Econometrics 208(1) https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2018.09.015 | Food-company stocks under-react to drought trends in their countries. | Supports our fertilizer result (CF+MOS underperform after El Niño), but the mechanism is untested. |
| Lanfear, Lioui & Siebert (2019), *Market anomalies and disaster risk: Evidence from extreme weather events*, J. Financial Markets https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2018.10.003 | Hurricane landfalls affect stock returns and anomalies. | Our calendar hurricane-season tests found nothing that survives (0 of 18). |

## B. Other non-obvious patterns
| Pattern | Key paper | Published claim | Our result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday/weekend effect | French (1980) JFE https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(80)90021-5 | Monday returns are negative. | Strong 1926–1979 (−25 bp/day). Post-publication it shrank to −6 bp in CRSP and is **absent in SPY/IWM since 1993**. This is a textbook decay. |
| Turn-of-month | Ariel (1987) JFE https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(87)90066-3 ; Lakonishok & Smidt (1988) RFS https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/1.4.403 | Most of the equity premium arrives on days −1..+3. | Still positive after 1988 (+6.3 bp/day, t=2.2 in CRSP). Weaker in SPY (t≈0.1 since 2005), and none in IWM. |
| Pre-holiday | Ariel (1990) JF https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb05110.x | Pre-holiday days return ~10× normal. | +31 bp in-sample, +7.8 bp after 1990 (t=1.5). Fading. |
| Pre-FOMC drift | Lucca & Moench (2015) JF https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12196 | Big excess return in the 24h before FOMC announcements. | +32 bp/day 1994–2010. **Since 2011, +12 bp (t=1.0): not significant.** Consistent with the post-publication decay others have reported. |
| Options-expiration week | Ni, Pearson & Poteshman (2005) JFE https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2004.08.005 | Pinning of stock prices near strikes at expiry. | No index-level return effect (t≈0). |
| Daylight saving | Kamstra, Kramer & Levi (2000) AER https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.90.4.1005 | Weekend after a DST change is a large negative. | Sign holds (−20 bp vs other Mondays) but t=−1.5. Not significant with only 115 events. |
| Moon phase (null check) | Yuan, Zheng & Zhu (2006) JEF https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jempfin.2005.06.001 | New-moon days beat full-moon days. | Null, as it should be (t=0.6). It works as our sanity check. |
| Halloween / Sell in May | Bouman & Jacobsen (2002) AER https://doi.org/10.1257/000282802762024683 | Nov–Apr beats May–Oct. | +0.56%/month, t=1.8, p=0.07. Does not survive correction. |
| Post-earnings drift | Bernard & Thomas (1989) JAR https://doi.org/10.2307/2491062 | Prices drift in the direction of the earnings surprise. | Long top-decile +2.1% over 63d (t=2.5, BH q=0.12). Short bottom-decile **loses money** (losers beat SPY), likely because of survivorship in our current-constituent universe. |
| Insider purchase clusters | Lakonishok & Lee (2001) RFS https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/14.1.79 ; Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski (2012) JF https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2012.01740.x | Insider buys predict returns, mostly in small caps; "opportunistic" insiders carry the signal. | In S&P 500/400 large caps: +1.6% over 63d after any purchase (t=2.4, q=0.15). Clusters are not stronger. Large caps are where the literature says the effect is weakest. |
| Congressional trades | Ziobrowski et al. (2004) JFQA https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109000003161 ; Eggers & Hainmueller (2013) JOP https://doi.org/10.1017/S002238161300011X ; Belmont et al. (2022) J. Public Econ. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2022.104696 | Early papers found big alpha. Later papers (2004–2008, 2012–2020 STOCK Act data) find **none**. | No edge from copying House trades after disclosure (2019–2026). Purchases of ≥$50k show +1.7% over 63d with t=1.7, but n=389, so it's noise-level. |
| Index additions | Shleifer (1986) JF https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1986.tb04518.x ; Greenwood & Sammon (2022) NBER w30748 https://www.nber.org/papers/w30748 | The S&P inclusion premium has largely disappeared. | No reliable post-inclusion reversal. |
| Google attention | Da, Engelberg & Gao (2011) JF https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2011.01679.x | Search spikes → short-run rise, then reversal. | **Not tested** (see data gaps). |
| Sports sentiment | Edmans, García & Norli (2007) JF https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2007.01262.x | National-team World Cup losses → next-day market drop. | **Not tested** (no free, clean match-result + country-index data assembled). |
| Post-publication decay (meta) | McLean & Pontiff (2016) JF https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365 | Anomaly returns fall ~58% after publication. | Our calendar results line up with this almost exactly. |

## C. Social media
| Paper | Claim | Our result |
|---|---|---|
| Bradley, Hanousek, Jame & Xiao (2024), *Place your bets? The value of investment research on Reddit's WallStreetBets*, RFS https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhad098 | Before GameStop, WSB due-diligence posts predicted returns. After the 2021 influx, the value disappeared. | 2021–2026 comment spikes predict **negative** next-day returns after costs (buying the spike loses ~0.9%/day, t=−3.0, survives BH). This is a reversal, not momentum. |
| Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz (2022), *Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users*, JF https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13183 | Retail herding into attention stocks is followed by negative returns. | Consistent with our WSB reversal. |
| Ge, Kurov & Wolfe (2019), *Do investors care about presidential company-specific tweets?*, J. Financial Research https://doi.org/10.1111/jfir.12177 | Trump's company tweets moved prices on the day. The effect was small and short-lived. | Our daily version (n=145 negative, 80 positive) shows no significant tradeable drift. Intraday data would be needed to see the day-0 effect properly. |
| Ante (2023), *How Elon Musk's Twitter activity moves cryptocurrency markets*, Tech. Forecasting & Social Change https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2022.122112 | Musk's tweets produce significant DOGE abnormal returns and volume within minutes. | Confirmed on the tweet day: **+3.7% DOGE vs BTC (t=2.8)**, +6.9% before June 2021 and +1.6% after. **From the next close on, nothing is left** (+0.3%, −0.5%), so you can't trade it on daily data. |
| Bollen, Mao & Zeng (2011), *Twitter mood predicts the stock market*, J. Comp. Sci. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jocs.2010.12.007 | 87% directional accuracy for the DJIA. | Widely failed to replicate. Not retested. |
